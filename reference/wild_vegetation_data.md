# Central Park Wild Area Vegetation Reference
## Botanical Data for 3D Simulation

Sources: NYBG Flora of Central Park survey (2013-2017), Linnaean Society botany walks,
Central Park Conservancy restoration records, iNaturalist observations, NYC Parks planting guides.

**Overall flora**: 438 spontaneous species, 262 genera, 89 families.
45% native (198 spp), 54% non-native (240 spp).
Largest families: Poaceae (56 spp), Asteraceae (55), Rosaceae (27), Fabaceae (20).

---

## VEGETATION LAYERS (for 3D vertical diversity)

The wild areas have 5 distinct visual layers:
1. **Ground cover** (0-0.3m): ferns, sedges, low wildflowers, leaf litter
2. **Herbaceous layer** (0.3-1.5m): wildflowers, tall ferns, grasses
3. **Tall herbaceous/invasive** (1.5-3m): knotweed, mugwort, pokeweed, Joe Pye weed
4. **Shrub layer** (2-5m): spicebush, viburnum, witch hazel, elderberry
5. **Understory tree** (5-12m): dogwood, redbud, serviceberry, hornbeam

## UNDERSTORY SHRUBS (2-5m layer, highest visual impact)

### Spicebush (Lindera benzoin) -- DOMINANT UNDERSTORY
- **Height**: 2-4m (6-12 ft)
- **Habit**: Multi-stemmed, colonial, forms clumps via root sprouting
- **Location**: Ramble, North Woods, Hallett -- forest interior
- **Visual**: Glossy aromatic leaves, open rounded form. Tiny yellow-green flowers before leaves (early spring). Bright red drupes on female plants (fall). Brilliant yellow fall color.
- **Season**: Spring flowers (March-April), red berries (Sept-Oct), yellow fall foliage
- **3D notes**: THE signature Central Park understory shrub. Open, spreading form in heavy shade. Zigzag branching pattern. Dense enough to partially obstruct sight lines on trails.

### Arrowwood Viburnum (Viburnum dentatum)
- **Height**: 2-3m (6-10 ft), can reach 4.5m
- **Habit**: Dense, multi-stemmed, rounded, upright then arching with age
- **Location**: Ramble, North Woods, Hallett -- woodland edges and interior
- **Visual**: Toothed glossy dark green leaves. White flat-topped flower clusters (late spring). Blue-black drupes (fall). Orange-red fall color.
- **Season**: White flowers (May-June), berries (Sept-Oct)
- **3D notes**: Denser than spicebush. Creates solid visual screens in understory.

### Witch Hazel (Hamamelis virginiana)
- **Height**: 3-5m (10-15 ft), occasionally to 9m
- **Habit**: Multi-stemmed, open irregular crown, vase-shaped
- **Location**: North Woods, Ramble, Hallett -- forest understory
- **Visual**: Large rounded leaves, zigzag branching in shade. Yellow strap-like flowers (Oct-Nov, among last to bloom). Clear yellow fall color. Thin smooth gray bark.
- **Season**: Fall flowers (unique -- blooms after leaf drop)
- **3D notes**: Larger than spicebush, can dominate mature understory. Distinctive zigzag architecture visible in winter.

### Sweet Pepperbush (Clethra alnifolia)
- **Height**: 1-2.5m (3-8 ft)
- **Habit**: Upright suckering shrub, forms thickets via suckers
- **Location**: Wet areas in Ramble, North Woods near streams/Loch
- **Visual**: White bottlebrush flower spikes (July-Aug). Yellow fall color. Exfoliating bark.
- **Season**: Summer flowers (July-Aug), fragrant
- **3D notes**: Wetland edge shrub. Smaller than spicebush. Clustered along stream banks.

### Elderberry (Sambucus canadensis)
- **Height**: 2-3.5m (5-12 ft)
- **Habit**: Multi-stemmed, arching, broader than tall, fast-growing
- **Location**: Woodland edges, stream banks, disturbed areas
- **Visual**: Compound leaves (5-7 leaflets). Large flat white flower clusters to 25cm across (June). Purple-black berry clusters (Aug-Sept).
- **Season**: Summer flowers, late summer berries
- **3D notes**: Arching form creates distinctive silhouette. Often at woodland/path edges.

### Purple Flowering Raspberry (Rubus odoratus)
- **Height**: 1-2m (3-6 ft), spreading to 3.5m wide
- **Habit**: Spreading shrub, no prickles, fast-growing
- **Location**: Woodland edges throughout Ramble and North Woods
- **Visual**: Large maple-like leaves (to 25cm). Rose-purple flowers 5cm across (summer). Hairy stems but no thorns. Yellow fall color.
- **Season**: Flowers June-August
- **3D notes**: Distinctive large palmate leaves. Spreading habit fills gaps at woodland edge. Easy to recognize.

### Staghorn Sumac (Rhus typhina)
- **Height**: 3-5m (10-16 ft)
- **Habit**: Multi-stemmed, flat-topped crown, forms colonies via root suckers
- **Location**: Rocky outcrops, woodland edges, open areas
- **Visual**: Large pinnately compound leaves (30+ leaflets). Velvety brown stems. Upright fuzzy red fruit clusters persist through winter. BRILLIANT red/orange/yellow fall color.
- **Season**: Year-round interest -- spring green, summer fruit, fall fire, winter architecture
- **3D notes**: Dramatic silhouette. Colony-forming creates groves. Velvet-covered antler-like branches.

## TALL HERBACEOUS PLANTS (1-3m, "wild" feel)

### Japanese Knotweed (Fallopia japonica) -- INVASIVE DOMINANT
- **Height**: 2-4.5m (6-15 ft)
- **Habit**: Dense bamboo-like thickets, hollow jointed stems, spreads by rhizomes
- **Location**: Stream banks, disturbed areas, edges throughout park
- **Visual**: Thick green stems with purple speckles and raised nodes (bamboo-like). Large heart-shaped leaves to 15cm. Creates impenetrable monoculture. White flower sprays (Aug-Sept). Dead brown canes persist through winter.
- **Season**: Peak July-Sept (explosive growth). Brown cane thickets persist all winter.
- **3D notes**: THE invasive that defines disturbed wild areas. Forms walls of vegetation 3m+ tall. Completely excludes other plants underneath. Dead winter canes are visually distinct.

### Pokeweed (Phytolacca americana) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 1.2-3m (4-10 ft)
- **Habit**: Branching, shrub-like, large taproot, dies back annually
- **Location**: Woodland edges, disturbed areas, gap openings
- **Visual**: MAGENTA/PURPLE stems are the signature feature (by August). Large elliptical leaves to 30cm. White-green flower racemes → dark purple-black berry clusters hanging in long bunches. Stems thick (to 5cm diameter).
- **Season**: Peak visual Aug-Oct (magenta stems + purple berries)
- **3D notes**: Extremely visually distinctive. The magenta stems against green foliage are unmistakable. Berries provide deep purple accents. Large, bold plant that fills gaps quickly.

### Joe Pye Weed (Eutrochium spp.) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 1.2-3m (4-10 ft)
- **Habit**: Erect, clump-forming, stout stems
- **Location**: Wet meadows, stream edges, Loch banks, pond margins
- **Visual**: Whorled lance leaves on thick stems. Large domed pink-purple flower heads (to 30cm across) at top. Stems often purple-tinged.
- **Season**: Peak bloom July-Sept
- **3D notes**: The classic tall wetland wildflower. Pink flower domes visible from distance. Often grows with ironweed, boneset, cardinal flower.

### Green-headed Coneflower (Rudbeckia laciniata) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 1.5-3m (5-10 ft)
- **Habit**: Tall, lanky, clump-forming, spreads by rhizomes to form colonies
- **Location**: Stream banks, wet meadows, floodplains, Loch area
- **Visual**: Deeply cut/lobed leaves. Yellow drooping ray petals around green cone center. Tall stalks branching in upper half.
- **Season**: Peak bloom July-Sept
- **3D notes**: Very tall -- stands above most other wildflowers. Yellow flowers on swaying stalks. Colonial spread creates patches.

### Mugwort (Artemisia vulgaris) -- INVASIVE DOMINANT
- **Height**: 0.6-1.5m (2-5 ft)
- **Habit**: Clumping perennial, woody stems, aggressive rhizome spread, forms monocultures
- **Location**: Open sunny areas, meadow edges, disturbed ground throughout park
- **Visual**: Deeply lobed dark green leaves, SILVERY-WHITE undersides (key identifier). Woody ridged stems. Inconspicuous flower clusters. Aromatic when crushed.
- **Season**: Foliage visible spring-fall, flowers late summer
- **3D notes**: Creates silvery-green patches in open areas. The white leaf undersides flash in wind -- distinctive visual effect. Often dominates open sunny zones before restoration.

### New York Ironweed (Vernonia noveboracensis) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 1.2-2.4m (4-8 ft)
- **Habit**: Erect, stiff stems, clump-forming
- **Location**: Wet meadows, stream banks, pond edges
- **Visual**: Deep purple fluffy disc flowers in loose terminal clusters. Lance leaves on greenish-purple stems. Rusty seed clusters persist.
- **Season**: Peak bloom July-Sept, rusty seeds into winter
- **3D notes**: Intense purple color. Grows with Joe Pye weed but flowers are darker. Stiff upright habit creates vertical accents.

### Rose Mallow (Hibiscus moscheutos) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 1-2m (3-7 ft)
- **Habit**: Erect, largely unbranched, dies back to ground
- **Location**: Pond edges, wet areas, wetland margins
- **Visual**: ENORMOUS dinner-plate flowers 15-30cm across -- white, pink, or red with dark eye. Tropical-looking broad leaves.
- **Season**: Peak bloom July-Sept
- **3D notes**: Show-stopping individual flowers. Each bloom is huge and unmistakable. Sparse plant structure with dramatic blooms.

### Boneset (Eupatorium perfoliatum) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 0.6-1.2m (2-4 ft)
- **Habit**: Erect, unbranched, clump-forming
- **Location**: Wetland edges, stream banks, pond margins
- **Visual**: Distinctive perfoliate leaves (appear to wrap around stem). White fuzzy flat-topped flower clusters. Hairy stems.
- **Season**: Peak bloom July-Sept
- **3D notes**: White flower clusters create a hazy effect. Perfoliate leaves are unique identifier. Often in groups near water.

## MEDIUM HERBACEOUS / WILDFLOWERS (0.3-1.2m)

### White Wood Aster (Eurybia divaricata) -- NATIVE, WOODLAND DOMINANT
- **Height**: 0.3-0.6m (1-2 ft)
- **Habit**: Loose clumps, spreads by rhizomes, sprawling dark stems
- **Location**: North Woods, Ramble, Hallett -- shaded forest floor
- **Visual**: Heart-shaped toothed leaves on dark zigzag stems. Flat-topped clusters of small white flowers with yellow-to-red disc centers. Covers woodland floor.
- **Season**: Peak bloom Sept-Nov (late, important for fall)
- **3D notes**: THE woodland floor wildflower in Central Park. Often dominant herbaceous species in the shade. Creates a white carpet effect in autumn.

### White Snakeroot (Ageratina altissima) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 0.5-1.5m (1.5-5 ft)
- **Habit**: Erect, branching, colonial via rhizomes, self-seeds prolifically
- **Location**: Woodland edges, shady clearings, North Woods, Ramble
- **Visual**: Nettle-like toothed heart-shaped leaves. Small fluffy bright white flower clusters in flat-topped corymbs. Blooms persist past frost.
- **Season**: Peak bloom Aug-Oct (late season)
- **3D notes**: White flowers glow in shade. Forms colonies at woodland edges. Taller than wood aster, often behind it at edges.

### Wild Bergamot / Bee Balm (Monarda fistulosa) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 0.6-1.2m (2-4 ft)
- **Habit**: Clumping, square stems, spreading
- **Location**: Meadow areas, Dene Slope, woodland edges
- **Visual**: Globe-shaped lavender-pink flower heads with showy bracts. Grey-green aromatic leaves on square stems. Oregano scent.
- **Season**: Peak bloom June-Sept
- **3D notes**: Distinctive globular flower heads. Often in meadow plantings. Grey-green foliage reads differently from surrounding plants.

### Cardinal Flower (Lobelia cardinalis) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 0.6-1m (2-3.5 ft)
- **Habit**: Erect, unbranched, clump-forming
- **Location**: Stream edges, Loch, wet woodland margins, Dene Slope
- **Visual**: BRILLIANT SCARLET red tubular flowers on tall spikes. Dark green to bronzy lance leaves.
- **Season**: Peak bloom July-Sept
- **3D notes**: The most intensely red native wildflower. Unmistakable color -- stands out from all surroundings. Key hummingbird plant.

### Blue Lobelia (Lobelia siphilitica) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 0.6-1m (2-3 ft)
- **Habit**: Erect, unbranched, clumping
- **Location**: Stream edges, wet areas, alongside cardinal flower
- **Visual**: Blue tubular flowers on spikes. Similar form to cardinal flower but blue.
- **Season**: Peak bloom Aug-Oct
- **3D notes**: Blue complement to cardinal flower's red. Often growing nearby in same wet areas.

### Jewelweed / Touch-me-not (Impatiens capensis) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 0.6-1.5m (2-5 ft)
- **Habit**: Annual, branching, forms dense stands along water
- **Location**: Stream banks, Loch, moist shady areas, Ramble stream
- **Visual**: Translucent succulent pale green stems. Orange-yellow spotted flowers shaped like tiny cornucopias. Seed pods explode when touched.
- **Season**: Peak bloom July-Sept
- **3D notes**: Creates lush green walls along streams. Translucent stems glow when backlit. Dense pure stands in floodplain areas. Important for "stream bank vegetation" look.

### Woodland Sunflower (Helianthus divaricatus) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 0.6-1.5m (2-5 ft)
- **Habit**: Erect, branching, rhizomatous colonies
- **Location**: Woodland edges, clearings, North Woods
- **Visual**: Small yellow sunflower heads. Opposite rough leaves. Branching habit.
- **Season**: Peak bloom July-Sept
- **3D notes**: Yellow accents at woodland edges. Not as tall as green-headed coneflower.

## FERNS (ground layer, 0.3-1.5m)

### Ostrich Fern (Matteuccia struthiopteris)
- **Height**: 1-1.8m (3-6 ft)
- **Habit**: Vase-shaped clumps, spreads by rhizomes, colonial
- **Location**: Moist woodland, stream banks, North Woods near Loch
- **Visual**: TALLEST fern. Bright green plume-like fronds form elegant vase shape. Separate dark brown fertile fronds persist through winter.
- **Season**: Peak May-Oct (green), brown fertile fronds year-round
- **3D notes**: Architectural vase form is very recognizable. Creates dramatic vertical accents in moist areas. The go-to "tall fern" for wild feel.

### Christmas Fern (Polystichum acrostichoides)
- **Height**: 0.3-0.6m (1-2 ft)
- **Habit**: Clumping rosette, EVERGREEN
- **Location**: Rocky slopes, woodland floor throughout Ramble, North Woods
- **Visual**: Glossy deep green lance-shaped fronds. Leathery texture. Stays green through winter (Christmas name).
- **Season**: Year-round (evergreen)
- **3D notes**: Important for winter green in woodland. Low, neat clumps on rocky slopes and beside paths.

### Cinnamon Fern (Osmundastrum cinnamomeum)
- **Height**: 0.6-1.2m (2-4 ft), can reach 1.8m with moisture
- **Habit**: Vase-shaped clumps, forms large colonies
- **Location**: Wet woodland, stream banks, boggy areas
- **Visual**: Blue-green sterile fronds in vase shape. Distinctive CINNAMON-BROWN fertile fronds stand erect in center. Yellow fall color.
- **Season**: Peak May-Oct, cinnamon fronds stand out in spring
- **3D notes**: The cinnamon-colored fertile fronds are unique visual signature. Grows larger in wetter spots than Christmas fern.

### Sensitive Fern (Onoclea sensibilis)
- **Height**: 0.3-1m (1-3.5 ft)
- **Habit**: Spreading via rhizomes, forms colonies, deciduous
- **Location**: Wet woodland, floodplains, stream banks
- **Visual**: Broad triangular fronds (unusual for fern). Coarse texture. Separate beaded fertile fronds. Dies back completely in winter.
- **Season**: Peak May-Sept (very frost-sensitive)
- **3D notes**: Coarser, less "ferny" than others. Broad fronds create different texture. Good for wet areas.

### Marginal Wood Fern (Dryopteris marginalis)
- **Height**: 0.3-0.6m (1-2 ft)
- **Habit**: Clumping crown, semi-evergreen
- **Location**: Rocky woodland, outcrops, Ramble boulders
- **Visual**: Blue-green leathery fronds in neat rosette. Fronds emerge as fiddleheads in spring.
- **Season**: Year-round (semi-evergreen)
- **3D notes**: Found on and around rock outcrops. Creates neat rosettes on boulders and steep slopes.

## GRASSES AND SEDGES (0.3-1.5m, meadow texture)

### Bottlebrush Grass (Elymus hystrix) -- NATIVE WOODLAND GRASS
- **Height**: 0.6-1.4m (2.5-4.5 ft)
- **Habit**: Cool-season, tufted clumps, shade-tolerant
- **Location**: Woodland floor, under oaks, North Woods
- **Visual**: Narrow grey-green leaves. Distinctive bottlebrush-shaped seed heads that mature to straw color. Persists into fall.
- **Season**: Seed heads June-Oct
- **3D notes**: The woodland shade grass. Bottlebrush seed heads are identifiable. Creates airy texture at knee height.

### Carex sedges (multiple species, 7+ in Central Park)
- **Height**: 0.3-0.9m (1-3 ft)
- **Habit**: Clumping or spreading, triangular stems
- **Location**: Wetland edges, woodland floor, stream banks
- **Visual**: Grass-like but with solid triangular stems. Various textures from fine to coarse. Arching leaf blades.
- **Key species**: C. blanda (woodland), C. lurida (wetland), C. vulpinoidea (wet meadow)
- **3D notes**: Finer texture than grasses. Essential for wetland/stream edge realism. Often form tussocks.

### Native meadow grasses (Dene Slope species)
- Little bluestem, switchgrass, and other cool-season species
- **Height**: 0.5-1.5m (1.5-5 ft)
- **Habit**: Bunch grasses, warm and cool season mix
- **Location**: Dene Slope, meadow areas, open sunny zones
- **3D notes**: Create the swaying meadow texture. Golden-tan fall/winter color.

## INVASIVE VINES (dramatic visual impact)

### Porcelain Berry (Ampelopsis brevipedunculata)
- **Height**: Climbs to 6m+ (20+ ft) in a single season
- **Habit**: Deciduous woody vine with tendrils, extremely aggressive
- **Location**: Throughout wild areas, climbing trees at woodland edges
- **Visual**: Heart-shaped deeply lobed leaves. MULTICOLORED berries in fall: white → yellow → lilac → green → turquoise → blue (uniquely colorful). Covers tree canopies.
- **Season**: Berries Sept-Nov, vine mass year-round
- **3D notes**: Creates heavy curtains of foliage over trees. Dead vine tangles visible in winter. The colorful berries are unique.

### Oriental Bittersweet (Celastrus orbiculatus)
- **Height**: Climbs to 18m+ (60 ft)
- **Habit**: Woody twining vine, wraps tightly around trunks, kills host trees
- **Location**: Throughout woodland edges, climbing canopy trees
- **Visual**: Round glossy leaves. Yellow outer fruit membranes split open to reveal bright red berries (fall/winter). Vine wrapping visible on trunks. Light brown bark with cracked texture.
- **Season**: Red/yellow fruits Oct-Feb persist. Vine structure visible year-round.
- **3D notes**: Visible as thick ropy vines on tree trunks. Fruit clusters eye-catching in fall/winter.

### Poison Ivy (Toxicodendron radicans)
- **Height**: Ground cover to climbing vine reaching tree canopy (20m+)
- **Habit**: Variable -- ground cover, shrub, or aerial vine with hairy rootlets
- **Location**: Throughout all wild areas, trail edges
- **Visual**: "Leaves of three" -- glossy trifoliate leaves. BRILLIANT red/orange fall color. Hairy aerial rootlets on climbing stems. White berries.
- **Season**: Year-round (hairy vine visible in winter)
- **3D notes**: Hairy vine trunks on trees are visually distinct. Excellent fall color. Ground cover form fills gaps.

## WETLAND / POND EDGE PLANTS

### Cattail (Typha latifolia)
- **Height**: 1.5-3m (5-10 ft)
- **Habit**: Colonial via rhizomes, forms dense stands in shallow water
- **Location**: Pond edges, Harlem Meer, The Pool, Turtle Pond margins
- **Visual**: Tall sword-like leaves. Iconic brown "hot dog" flower spikes. Dense stands.
- **Season**: Year-round (brown spikes persist through winter)
- **3D notes**: THE wetland indicator plant. Instantly reads as "pond edge." Brown spikes unmistakable.

### Yellow Flag Iris (Iris pseudacorus) -- INVASIVE
- **Height**: 0.6-1.5m (2-5 ft)
- **Habit**: Clumping, rhizomatous, forms dense stands in shallow water/mud
- **Location**: Pond edges, lake margins throughout park
- **Visual**: Sword-shaped dark green leaves with raised midrib. Bright yellow flowers 7-10cm (late spring). Dense clumps at water's edge.
- **Season**: Peak bloom May-June, foliage spring-fall
- **3D notes**: Bright yellow flowers are eye-catching at pond edges. Sword leaves create vertical lines.

### Lizard's Tail (Saururus cernuus) -- NATIVE
- **Height**: 0.6-1.2m (2-4 ft)
- **Habit**: Colonial, spreads in shallow water and moist mud
- **Location**: Stream edges, Loch, pond margins
- **Visual**: Heart-shaped/arrowhead rich green leaves. Distinctive drooping white flower spikes that curve at tip (the "lizard's tail"). Fragrant.
- **Season**: Peak bloom June-Sept
- **3D notes**: The drooping curved flower spikes are unique. Creates patches along stream edges.

### Common Reed / Phragmites (Phragmites australis) -- INVASIVE
- **Height**: 2-5m (6-16 ft)
- **Habit**: Dense impenetrable monoculture stands, rhizomatous
- **Location**: Wetland margins, pond edges (invasive, being managed)
- **Visual**: Tall rigid tan stems. Large feathery purplish-brown seed heads. Leaves 30-60cm. Creates reed beds.
- **Season**: Peak visual late summer-winter (feathery plumes). Dead tan stems persist.
- **3D notes**: Where present, dominates completely. Creates walls of reeds. Feathery plumes sway in wind.

### Duckweed (Lemna spp.) and Watermeal (Wolffia spp.)
- **Height**: Floating surface (0m)
- **Habit**: Floating colonies covering still water surface
- **Location**: Still pond areas, The Pond, still water edges
- **Visual**: Tiny green dots/leaves covering water surface. Creates bright green carpet.
- **Season**: Spring-fall
- **3D notes**: Important for water surface texture. Partial coverage reads as stagnant/wild water.

## LOCATION-SPECIFIC VEGETATION CHARACTER

### The Ramble (36 acres, X=-400,Z=600)
- **Character**: Designed "wild garden," winding paths through dense woodland
- **Canopy**: Oaks (white, black, red, scarlet, willow), sycamore, hackberry, tulip tree
- **Understory**: Spicebush dominant, viburnum, witch hazel
- **Ground layer**: White wood aster, Christmas fern, bottlebrush grass
- **Stream areas**: Jewelweed along Gill, ferns on banks
- **Edges/disturbed**: Pokeweed, mugwort patches, porcelain berry on trees
- **Invasive pressure**: Porcelain berry, bittersweet on canopy trees

### North Woods (90 acres, X=600,Z=-1315)
- **Character**: True deciduous forest, the most natural area in park
- **Canopy**: Oak-hickory-maple-ash forest
- **Understory**: Spicebush, witch hazel, elderberry
- **Ground layer**: White wood aster, woodland goldenrod, Allegheny spurge, leaf litter
- **The Loch**: Ferns on banks, jewelweed, cardinal flower, lizard's tail
- **Ravine meadow**: Coneflower, cup plant, bee balm, goldenrods, asters, native grasses
- **Stream banks**: Ostrich fern, cinnamon fern, sensitive fern

### Hallett Nature Sanctuary (4 acres)
- **Character**: Restored woodland, smallest wild area, was overrun with invasives until 2016
- **Restored with**: Native plants replacing wisteria and Japanese knotweed
- **Notable**: Dutchman's breeches, shooting stars, azaleas, columbines, Jack-in-the-pulpit
- **Understory**: Native shrubs post-restoration

### Pond/Lake Edges
- **Hard edges**: Most water bodies have artificially hard edges (stone, concrete)
- **Naturalized edges**: Cattails, yellow flag iris, lizard's tail where allowed
- **Persistent species**: Bidens, buttonbush, boneset, rose mallow, jewelweed, native sedges
- **The Pond (south)**: Restored wetland plantings with seasonal color
- **Harlem Meer**: Bald cypress along edge, some cattail stands

### Dene Slope (meadow)
- **Character**: Managed native wildflower meadow, 50+ species
- **Visual**: Tall grasses and wildflowers, seasonal succession of bloom
- **Height**: 0.3-1.5m meadow, swaying in wind

## SEASONAL VISUAL CALENDAR

### SPRING (March-May)
- Spicebush tiny yellow flowers (before leaves)
- Fern fiddleheads unfurling
- Dutchman's breeches, shooting stars (Hallett)
- Jack-in-the-pulpit in deep shade
- Yellow flag iris at pond edges
- Serviceberry white flowers
- Redbud pink flowers in understory

### SUMMER (June-August)
- Joe Pye weed pink domes at wetland edges
- Cardinal flower scarlet spikes along streams
- Jewelweed orange along stream banks (dense)
- Wild bergamot lavender globes in meadows
- Elderberry white then purple-black
- Rose mallow huge pink/white blooms at pond edges
- Japanese knotweed reaches full 3m+ height
- Pokeweed magenta stems develop
- Phragmites plumes emerge
- Sweet pepperbush white spikes (fragrant)

### FALL (September-November)
- WHITE WOOD ASTER blankets woodland floor
- Goldenrod + aster display in meadows (yellow + purple)
- Pokeweed purple-black berry clusters
- Porcelain berry multicolored fruits
- Bittersweet red/yellow fruits
- Staghorn sumac BRILLIANT red/orange
- Witch hazel yellow strap flowers (late)
- Spicebush red berries + yellow leaves
- Mugwort silvery patches in open areas
- Cattail brown spikes fully formed

### WINTER (December-February)
- Christmas fern and marginal wood fern stay green
- Ostrich fern brown fertile fronds persist
- Cattail brown spikes
- Japanese knotweed dead brown cane thickets
- Poison ivy hairy vines visible on trunks
- Bittersweet/porcelain berry dead vine tangles on trees
- Witch hazel last flowers, then bare zigzag branches
- Sumac red fruit clusters + antler architecture

## PRIORITY SPECIES FOR 3D SIMULATION (by visual impact)

### Tier 1 -- Must-have (define the wild character)
1. **Spicebush** -- dominant understory shrub everywhere
2. **White wood aster** -- dominant woodland floor wildflower
3. **Ostrich fern** -- tallest, most dramatic fern
4. **Christmas fern** -- evergreen ground cover on rocks

## MODELING NOTES

### Growth forms for 3D
- **Clump/bunch**: Most ferns, asters, grasses (radial from center)
- **Colonial/spreading**: Spicebush, knotweed, mugwort, ferns (patch-based placement)
- **Vine/climbing**: Porcelain berry, bittersweet, poison ivy (on tree trunks/canopy)
- **Erect spike**: Joe Pye, cardinal flower, ironweed (vertical emphasis)
- **Multi-stem shrub**: Viburnum, witch hazel, elderberry (branching from base)
- **Rosette**: Christmas fern, marginal wood fern (ground-hugging)

### Placement rules
- **Stream banks**: Jewelweed (dense), ferns, cardinal flower, lizard's tail, sedges
- **Woodland floor (shade)**: White wood aster, Christmas fern, bottlebrush grass
- **Woodland edge (part shade)**: Pokeweed, white snakeroot, purple flowering raspberry
- **Disturbed open areas**: Mugwort, burdock, knotweed patches
- **Pond edges**: Cattail, yellow flag iris, sedges, rose mallow
- **Wet meadow**: Joe Pye weed, ironweed, boneset, green-headed coneflower
- **Rock outcrops**: Christmas fern, marginal wood fern, staghorn sumac
- **Trees with vines**: Porcelain berry, bittersweet, poison ivy (aerial roots visible)
